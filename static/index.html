About this project

A self-learning project for Foundations of Financial Systems and AI Applications — Third Year B.Tech. AI & Data Science, Shah and Anchor Kutchhi Engineering College.

How it works

The system predicts whether a credit-card customer will default on next month's payment. It returns a low-risk or high-risk label together with an estimated probability, trained on 30,000 historical accounts from the UCI Default of Credit Card Clients dataset.

  1. Duplicate rows are removed, then data is split 70 / 15 / 15 into training, validation and test sets (stratified, seed 42).
  2. Imputation and scaling are fitted on the training split only, so no information leaks from validation or test.
  3. A majority-class baseline, Logistic Regression and Random Forest are tuned with 5-fold cross-validation. Gradient Boosting is trained as an extension for comparison.
  4. The model and decision threshold are chosen on the validation split. The test split is used exactly once for the final figures.

Limitations

Read these before interpreting any prediction.

Old, foreign data

Taiwanese credit-card accounts from 2005. Patterns may not hold for present-day or Indian borrowers.

Missing information

No income, employment or loan amount, so no debt-to-income ratio. Only six months of behaviour.

Moderate accuracy

It misses a substantial share of real defaults and also flags many customers who go on to pay.

Associations, not causes

Feature importance and "what moved this estimate" describe statistical patterns, not reasons a person defaults.

Fairness

Sex, education, marital status and age are not model inputs; they are used only to compare error rates. Similar rates do not prove fairness.

Privacy

Uses public, de-identified data. Nothing entered or uploaded is stored by the server.
